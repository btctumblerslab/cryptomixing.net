Crypto Mixers in 2026: What Actually Works for Bitcoin, Ethereum and Tokens

Bitcoin and Ethereum keep every transfer on a public record, and the tools that read that record have never been cheaper. [Crypto mixers](https://cryptomixing.net/) cut the line between where coins came from and where they go next. They do it in very different ways on each chain, so a tool that is great for BTC can be useless for ETH. This guide sorts six of them by chain, cost and custody risk, using terms and news checked this week.

How [Crypto Mixers](https://cryptomixing.net/) Work on Bitcoin and on Ethereum

Bitcoin moves in discrete coins called UTXOs. A payment spends some of them and creates new ones, and analysts follow those coins hop by hop. That is why a bitcoin mixer can work by simply swapping coins: you send yours in, you get back different coins that never touched your history, ideally later and in uneven pieces. CoinJoin does the same thing without a middleman by having many people sign one transaction with identical outputs.

Ethereum keeps balances per account instead. There are no coins to swap, only a number on an address that everyone can see grow and shrink. So privacy there means a shared pool contract: you deposit, the contract records a secret commitment, and later you prove with zero-knowledge math that you own one of the deposits without saying which. Fixed sizes such as 1 ETH make deposits look alike; encrypted notes, as in Railgun, let any amount blend.

Whatever the chain, the strength of any mix is the crowd you hide in and the habits you keep afterwards. A pool with five users protects nobody, however clever the math.
